7 Home Security Upgrades Every Orlando Homeowner Should Make in 2026

Home burglary rates in Orange County have declined over the past decade, but break-ins remain a real risk — and the methods criminals use have evolved. Older security advice (buy a dog, get an alarm sign) is no longer sufficient when the most common forced entry takes under 60 seconds against a standard residential door.

These seven upgrades are ranked by cost-effectiveness: the highest security gain per dollar spent. Most can be done in a single weekend afternoon. A few require a licensed locksmith — but those are the ones that make the biggest difference.

Quick stat: 34% of home burglars enter through the front door (FBI UCR). Of those, the majority succeed by kicking in the door frame rather than defeating the lock. The most impactful upgrades address the door frame and strike plate — not just the lock hardware.
1
Reinforced Strike Plate $15–$25 Highest Impact/Cost Ratio

The strike plate is the metal bracket the deadbolt enters when the door closes. A standard strike plate uses two ¾-inch screws that grip only the door trim — not the stud behind it. One solid kick separates it from the frame.

A reinforced strike plate (sometimes called a door armor plate or security strike) uses 4–6 screws, 3 inches long, that anchor directly into the wall stud. The hardware costs $15–$25 and is available at any home improvement store. It is the single highest-impact security upgrade per dollar in residential security.

Install on: Every exterior door, including the door connecting your garage to the living area.

2
Grade 1 Deadbolt Upgrade $40–$100 + install High Impact

The ANSI/BHMA grading system rates residential locks on three levels. Grade 3 is the minimum — common in builder-grade homes and widely sold at discount retailers. Grade 1 is the highest residential rating, tested against significantly higher physical attack forces.

If your home has Grade 2 or Grade 3 deadbolts on exterior doors, upgrading to Grade 1 hardware from Schlage (B60N series), Kwikset (980 series), or Medeco is a meaningful improvement. The cost difference between a Grade 3 and Grade 1 deadbolt is often $30–$50.

A licensed locksmith can assess your current hardware grade and install upgraded deadbolts in the same visit as a rekey.

3
Sliding Door Secondary Lock $10–$40 High Impact for FL Homes

Sliding glass doors are one of the most common entry points in Florida home burglaries. The factory latch on most sliding doors provides minimal resistance to someone lifting and prying the door from its track.

Add two layers of security: a bar lock or wooden dowel cut to fit the track (prevents horizontal movement) and a pin lock or charley bar through the door frame (prevents the door from being lifted). Total cost: under $40 for both. This upgrade takes 20 minutes and requires no professional installation.

4
Smart Lock with Activity Monitoring $150–$425 installed High Convenience + Security

Smart locks add capabilities that mechanical locks cannot provide: remote locking confirmation, temporary access codes for guests and contractors, activity logs showing every entry, and low-battery alerts.

For Orlando homeowners — particularly those with vacation rental properties, frequent housekeepers, or contractors entering during the day — a smart lock eliminates the need for physical key distribution entirely. Time-limited codes expire automatically, so a guest code from last month cannot be used this month.

5
Video Doorbell + Exterior Cameras $100–$500 Strong Deterrent

Visible security cameras are one of the most effective deterrents in residential security research — not because they prevent crime through surveillance, but because they signal to a prospective intruder that evidence will exist. Most residential burglars specifically seek low-risk targets.

A video doorbell (Ring, Google Nest, Eufy) covers the primary entry point and allows remote visitor verification. Adding one or two exterior cameras covering the backyard and garage driveway completes the visible coverage. Ensure cameras are mounted in weather-protected positions — Orlando's summer storms are hard on electronics.

6
Door Frame Reinforcement Kit $50–$150 High Impact

For maximum kick-in resistance, a door frame reinforcement kit (brands include Door Armor, StrikeMaster II, and Armor Concepts) wraps the hinge side, lock side, and door edge in heavy-gauge steel. Combined with a reinforced strike plate and Grade 1 deadbolt, this configuration is nearly impossible to defeat by brute force.

These kits are available at home improvement stores and can be installed with standard tools in 1–2 hours per door. For rental properties where tenant safety is a liability concern, door frame reinforcement is one of the most defensible investments a landlord can make.

7
High-Security Cylinders for Primary Entry $80–$200 per cylinder + install Maximum Pick/Bump Resistance

Standard residential lock cylinders — including most Grade 1 deadbolts — can be defeated by lock picking or bump keys in the hands of a skilled attacker. High-security cylinders from Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, or ASSA ABLOY use patented pin configurations that make picking and bumping significantly more difficult.

High-security cylinders also use restricted keyways — keys cannot be duplicated at a standard hardware store. Any key copy requires written authorization from the registered owner. This is particularly valuable for homes where key control is a long-term concern: rental properties, shared ownership, or frequent housekeeping access.

A licensed locksmith in Orlando can assess which entry points warrant high-security cylinders based on your specific property layout, existing hardware, and risk profile — and install them alongside any of the upgrades above.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most cost-effective home security upgrade?

Replacing the strike plate on all exterior doors with a reinforced version using 3-inch screws is the highest-impact, lowest-cost upgrade — under $25 per door. Combined with a Grade 1 deadbolt, this defeats the most common residential forced-entry method.

How much does smart lock installation cost in Orlando?

Smart lock hardware costs $80–$300 depending on brand. Professional installation in Orlando runs $75–$125 in labor. Total installed cost: $150–$425. A licensed locksmith ensures proper door prep and alignment.

Do home security upgrades lower homeowner's insurance in Florida?

Yes. Many Florida insurers offer discounts of 2–15% for documented security upgrades including deadbolt installation, alarm systems, and smart locks. Request a written service record from your locksmith to submit to your insurance provider.
