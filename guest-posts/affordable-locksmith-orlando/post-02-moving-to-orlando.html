Moving to Orlando, FL? Your Complete New Home Security Checklist (2026)

Relocating to Central Florida is exciting — the weather, the lifestyle, and the housing market all make Orlando one of the fastest-growing metros in the country. But in the flurry of closing paperwork, moving trucks, and utility transfers, one of the most important steps gets skipped by nearly every new homeowner: securing the locks before you unpack a single box.

This checklist covers everything you need to do in the first 48 hours, the first week, and the first month to ensure your new Orlando home is genuinely secure — not just superficially so.

Before You Move In: The 48-Hour Security Window

Step 1 — Rekey Every Exterior Lock Immediately

Quick Answer: Rekeying should happen before your first night in the home. A three-lock rekey in Orlando costs $100–$175 and takes under an hour. This single action eliminates all security risk from previous key holders.

By the time you close on a home in Florida, an average of 8–12 people have held a key to the property: the previous owner, their family members, contractors, the listing agent, the buyer's agent, the property manager (if it was a rental), cleaning services, and inspectors. None of these keys are collected at closing.

Rekeying changes the internal pin configuration of your existing lock cylinders so that every previously cut key becomes useless. The hardware stays in place — only the keys change. It is faster, cheaper, and equally as effective as replacing the locks entirely when the hardware is in good condition.

What to rekey:

  • Front door deadbolt and knob/handle lock
  • Back door and side door
  • Attached garage entry door (the door between garage and living space)
  • Any exterior gate locks
  • Mailbox lock if it uses a key

Step 2 — Assess Your Deadbolt Quality

While the locksmith is on-site for rekeying, ask them to assess the deadbolt security grade on each exterior door. Florida building codes allow Grade 2 deadbolts as a minimum, but Grade 1 (ANSI/BHMA) provides meaningfully stronger resistance to kick-in attacks, picking, and bump keys.

If your home has Grade 3 deadbolts on exterior doors — common in tract homes and budget construction — consider upgrading to Grade 1 hardware from Schlage, Kwikset, or Medeco at the same visit.

Step 3 — Change the Garage Door Code

Garage doors with a keypad entry retain the code programmed by the previous owner. Change it before your first night. Refer to your garage door opener's manual — most models allow you to reset the PIN from the interior wall console in under two minutes.

Also check whether your garage door opener supports rolling code technology (also called Security+ 2.0 or similar). Older fixed-code systems can be cloned with inexpensive remote devices. If your opener is more than 10 years old, replacement is worth considering.

During Your First Week: Upgrade and Assess

Florida-Specific Security Considerations

Orlando's climate and demographic profile create security considerations you may not have encountered in other states:

  • Humidity and salt air: Lock hardware corrodes faster in Florida than in most of the continental US. Inspect exterior locks annually and apply a silicone-based lubricant. Stiff or sticky locks are a security vulnerability as well as an inconvenience.
  • Storm shutters and hurricane prep: If your home has storm shutters, verify that the locking mechanisms function correctly before storm season begins in June.
  • Sliding glass doors: Standard sliding door locks are notoriously weak. Add a secondary bar lock (a cut-down wooden dowel or a purpose-built bar) in the track, and consider a secondary pin lock through the frame.
  • High transient population: Orlando's tourism economy means a higher-than-average rate of short-term residents, vacation rental turnovers, and service workers moving through residential neighborhoods. Good exterior lighting and clearly visible security signage are meaningful deterrents.

Strike Plate Upgrade

The weakest point of most residential doors is not the lock itself but the strike plate — the metal bracket the bolt enters when the door closes. A standard strike plate held by ¾-inch screws pulls free with one or two kicks. Replacing it with a reinforced strike plate using 3-inch screws that reach the wall stud costs under $20 in hardware and takes 15 minutes to install.

Your Complete Move-In Security Checklist

48-Hour Checklist

  • ☐ Rekey all exterior door locks before first night
  • ☐ Change garage door keypad PIN
  • ☐ Locate and secure all spare keys (check under mats, in lockboxes, on hooks)
  • ☐ Test all window and sliding door locks
  • ☐ Add secondary bar to all sliding glass doors

First Week Checklist

  • ☐ Assess deadbolt grades — upgrade to Grade 1 on all exterior doors if needed
  • ☐ Replace strike plates with reinforced versions on all exterior doors
  • ☐ Install smart lock(s) on primary entry points if desired
  • ☐ Set up exterior lighting (motion-activated at minimum)
  • ☐ Check for garage door rolling code technology

First Month Checklist

  • ☐ Install video doorbell or exterior cameras
  • ☐ Register new address with local non-emergency police (vacation property watch programs)
  • ☐ Review homeowner's insurance for security discount requirements
  • ☐ Document all lock hardware (brand, model, grade) for insurance records
  • ☐ Set calendar reminder for annual lock lubrication and inspection

Finding a Reliable Locksmith in Orlando for Your Move-In Service

The move-in rekey should be the first call you make after receiving your keys at closing. When selecting a locksmith in Orlando, verify three things before booking: a current Florida DACS (Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services) license, general liability insurance, and an upfront price quote.

Florida-licensed locksmiths are required to provide their license number on request. Do not allow work to begin until you have verified it. The DACS license lookup is available at the Florida government website.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I rekey my locks when I move into a new home in Orlando?

Yes — always. Previous owners, agents, contractors, and inspectors all had keys to your new home. Rekeying before you move in ensures nobody outside your household can enter. It costs $25–$45 per lock cylinder, takes under an hour for a full home, and can be done same-day by a licensed locksmith in Orlando.

How much does it cost to rekey a house in Orlando, FL?

Rekeying a standard Orlando home with 3–5 exterior locks costs $100–$175 total. This is significantly less than replacing all locks ($400–$800+) and delivers the same security result when the existing hardware is in good condition.

What smart lock works best in Florida's humidity?

The Schlage Encode Plus and Kwikset Halo are both rated for outdoor use and perform well in Florida's humidity. Both feature built-in Wi-Fi (no hub required), Grade 1 ANSI/BHMA security ratings, and battery backup in case of power outages.
